The other major activity was to drill holes for the windshield wiper mounts. Fortunately, I investigated this prior to beginning the work.Â  I found out that I had outdated instructions for the installation.Â  The new instructions required moving the position of the drilling guide tool.Â  I put this in the correct position for the Mark IV, and drilled the wiper mechanism mount holes on each side.

Prior to doing any of the body work, I first wanted to clean the outer surface of the body and door, trunk lid and hood panels to get rid of remnant mold release chemicals.Â  I went over it with a degreaser and with comet cleanser.Â 

Next I began working on the seams on the body.Â  This required that I first grind the seams to eliminate excess material.Â  I ground the seams just below the surface of the panels.Â  Along the seams, this created a groove or channel roughly 1/4 – 1/2″ wide and 1/16 – 1/8″ deep.Â  Next I had to build this back up to match the panels on either side and the other contours of the body.Â  I filled all the channels with Rage Gold filler.Â  After it dried I began sanding the excess material.Â  Most of this was done with 80 grit sandpaper and a sanding block.Â  Some of the very sharp curves were done with just the sandpaper.

After the first sanding was complete, I had my work inspected.Â  As I expected, Dave showed me some places that I needed to fill and or sand again where it was too high or too low or not the proper contour.Â  He said some places might need multiple steps.Â  I re-filled and sanded all the seams a second time.Â  This came out much better, but I did still find quite a few spots I wanted to do again.Â Â  These were filled and sanded, and the whole car checked again.Â  All told, I went over various sections of the seams up to 6 times to get the shaping right.Â  This included along door openings, the fuel filler opening and some of the rolled edges along the cockpit by the dash area and in the front opening of the body.Â  I also filled the entire underside of the hood scoop and sanded it several times.Â  This piece was essentially just fiberglas, and as it will be visible with the hood open, it needs a finished appearance.Â  Ultimately, I got this done to the best of my ability.Â  Any final work prior to primer will be done by Dave.

Once the work wit the filler was done, I needed to sand all the gelcoat surfaces of the body and the door, trunk and hood panels.Â  I did this using 180 grit sandpaper as the objective was to simply clean off all the shiny surface of the gelcoat.Â  That has all been completed, all pieces have been cleaned with compressed air, and now I am ready to mount the body on the chassis and start fitting the doors, trunk lid and hood in their respective openings.
